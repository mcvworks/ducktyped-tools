Device Lookup Explained

Last updated: March 13, 2026

Understand how IMEI numbers, Apple serial numbers, and Dell service tags work -- and how duckTyped's Device Lookup tool uses them to identify devices, manufacturers, and warranty status without sending your data to third parties.

What Is an IMEI Number?

An IMEI (International Mobile Equipment Identity) is a unique 15-digit number assigned to every mobile phone, tablet, and cellular-enabled device. It serves as the device's fingerprint on cellular networks -- carriers use it to identify devices, authorize network access, and block stolen phones from connecting.

The IMEI is assigned during manufacturing and is permanently tied to the device hardware. Unlike a phone number or SIM card, which can change, the IMEI stays the same regardless of which carrier or SIM is used. This makes it the most reliable way to identify a specific physical device.

Every IMEI is structured as follows:

  • Digits 1-8: TAC (Type Allocation Code) -- identifies the manufacturer and model. Assigned by the GSMA.
  • Digits 9-14: Serial Number -- a unique serial assigned by the manufacturer to distinguish individual units of the same model.
  • Digit 15: Check Digit -- calculated using the Luhn algorithm to detect transcription errors. Some older devices use only 14 digits and omit this check digit.

How TAC Identification Works

The TAC (Type Allocation Code) is the first 8 digits of an IMEI. It uniquely identifies the device type -- not just the brand, but the specific model, variant, and sometimes even the regional configuration. The GSMA maintains the global TAC database and assigns codes to manufacturers when they register new devices.

For example, a TAC might map to "Apple iPhone 14 Pro" or "Samsung Galaxy S23 Ultra". By looking up just the first 8 digits of an IMEI, you can determine exactly what device it belongs to without needing the full serial number.

The duckTyped Device Lookup tool loads a TAC database directly into your browser, so the lookup happens entirely client-side. Your IMEI never leaves your device.

Luhn Checksum Validation

The 15th digit of a standard IMEI is a check digit calculated using the Luhn algorithm (also known as the "modulus 10" algorithm). This is the same algorithm used to validate credit card numbers.

The Luhn check detects common transcription errors -- single mistyped digits, transposed adjacent digits, and other copying mistakes. If the Luhn checksum fails, the IMEI was almost certainly entered incorrectly. A valid Luhn check does not guarantee the IMEI is real or active -- it only confirms the number is internally consistent.

How to Find Your IMEI

There are several ways to find a device's IMEI number:

  • Dial *#06# -- on most phones, this instantly displays the IMEI. Works on both Android and iPhone.
  • Android: Settings > About Phone > IMEI (or Status > IMEI Information).
  • iPhone: Settings > General > About > scroll down to IMEI.
  • SIM tray: On many iPhones and some Android devices, the IMEI is engraved on the SIM card tray.
  • Original packaging: The IMEI is printed on the barcode label of the device box.
  • Under the battery: On older phones with removable batteries, the IMEI is on a label underneath.

Dual-SIM phones have two IMEI numbers -- one for each SIM slot. Both are valid and can be looked up independently.

What Is an Apple Serial Number?

Apple assigns a serial number to every product it manufactures -- iPhones, iPads, Macs, Apple Watches, AirPods, and accessories. Until late 2021, these serials followed a structured 12-character format that encoded manufacturing details.

Pre-2021 Format (12 Characters)

The traditional Apple serial number is 12 alphanumeric characters, structured as:

  • Characters 1-3: Factory Code -- identifies the manufacturing facility (e.g., "C02" for a Quanta facility in China, "F5K" for a Foxconn plant).
  • Character 4: Year + Half -- a single character encoding the year and whether the device was made in the first or second half of the year.
  • Character 5: Week -- a character encoding the manufacturing week within that half-year.
  • Characters 6-8: Unique Identifier -- distinguishes individual units produced in the same week at the same factory.
  • Characters 9-12: Model Code -- a 4-character code identifying the exact product configuration (model, color, storage capacity, etc.).

This format allows the serial to be decoded into useful manufacturing information, which is exactly what the duckTyped tool does.

Post-2021 Format (10 Characters)

Starting with products released in late 2021, Apple switched to randomized 10-character serial numbers. These serials are purely random identifiers with no embedded manufacturing data. Apple made this change for privacy reasons and to prevent serial number prediction or enumeration.

Post-2021 serials cannot be decoded offline. You can still use them with Apple's official support tools, but third-party tools (including ours) cannot extract factory, date, or model information from the new format. For these devices, IMEI lookup is a better alternative.

What Is a Dell Service Tag?

A Dell service tag is a unique 5-7 character alphanumeric code assigned to every Dell computer, monitor, and peripheral. It serves as the primary identifier for Dell's warranty and support systems.

The service tag links the device to its specific configuration (model, CPU, RAM, storage, etc.) and warranty coverage. Dell's support API returns the product model and all associated warranty entitlements when queried with a valid service tag.

How to Find Your Dell Service Tag

  • Physical label: On laptops, look at the bottom of the device. On desktops, check the top or side panel. On monitors, check the back.
  • BIOS/UEFI: The service tag is displayed on the BIOS splash screen during boot.
  • Windows Command Prompt: Run wmic bios get serialnumber to display the service tag.
  • PowerShell: Run Get-CimInstance Win32_BIOS | Select SerialNumber.
  • Linux: Run sudo dmidecode -s system-serial-number.

Privacy: What Stays Local vs. What Gets Sent

The duckTyped Device Lookup tool is designed with privacy as a priority:

  • IMEI lookups are 100% client-side. The TAC database is loaded into your browser and all processing happens locally. Your IMEI never leaves your device.
  • Apple serial lookups are 100% client-side. The serial format database is loaded into your browser and decoded locally.
  • Dell service tag lookups require an API call. The service tag is sent to the duckTyped backend, which proxies the request to Dell's warranty API. No data is stored or logged on our servers -- the request is forwarded and the result is returned directly to your browser.
