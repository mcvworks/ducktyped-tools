SSL Certificate Revoked

Last updated: March 8, 2026

A revoked SSL certificate has been invalidated by the certificate authority before its expiry date. This is a serious security signal — do not bypass this warning.

You might see this error as:

  • SSL certificate revoked
  • ERR_CERT_REVOKED
  • Certificate has been revoked
  • OCSP revoked
  • SEC_ERROR_REVOKED_CERTIFICATE

What Does This Error Mean?

Certificate authorities (CAs) maintain revocation lists. When a certificate is revoked, the CA adds it to its Certificate Revocation List (CRL) or marks it as revoked in OCSP (Online Certificate Status Protocol). Browsers check revocation status and block connections to sites using revoked certificates.

Chrome shows NET::ERR_CERT_REVOKED, Firefox shows SEC_ERROR_REVOKED_CERTIFICATE, and Safari shows "This certificate has been revoked by its CA."

Why Are Certificates Revoked?

  • Private key compromised — the certificate's private key was exposed, stolen, or leaked. The most serious reason.
  • Misissued certificate — the CA made an error in the certificate's domain validation process.
  • Domain ownership change — the previous owner requested revocation after transferring the domain.
  • Company dissolved — the organization that requested the certificate no longer exists.
  • Voluntary revocation — the site owner requested revocation for administrative reasons.
  • CA policy violation — the certificate was found to violate CA/Browser Forum baseline requirements.

How to Fix It

For Site Owners

1. Issue a New Certificate Immediately

Request a fresh certificate from your CA. For Let's Encrypt:

sudo certbot certonly --force-renew -d yourdomain.com

2. Rotate Your Private Key

If the revocation was due to key compromise, generate a new private key. Do not reuse the old key. Treat the old key as fully compromised and revoke any other certificates that used it.

3. Investigate the Cause

Determine why the certificate was revoked. Check your CA's portal for the revocation reason. If the cause was a key compromise, audit your server's security and credentials.

4. Implement OCSP Stapling

OCSP stapling improves revocation check performance. The server periodically fetches its own OCSP response and includes it in the TLS handshake, avoiding the browser needing to contact the CA.

For Visitors

Never proceed past a revocation warning. This error means a security authority has flagged the certificate as untrustworthy. Leave the site and contact the owner through another channel.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly does revocation take effect?

OCSP revocation is near real-time for browsers that check it. CRL-based revocation depends on the browser's CRL cache TTL. Chrome uses its own CRLSets (push-updated bundles) for high-priority revocations, which can take effect within hours.

Can a revoked certificate be un-revoked?

No. Certificate revocation is permanent. Once a certificate is added to a revocation list, it stays there until it naturally expires. You must obtain a new certificate.
